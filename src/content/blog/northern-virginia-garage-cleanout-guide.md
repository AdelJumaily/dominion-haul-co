Almost every garage in Northern Virginia reaches the same state, and it takes about twelve years to get there.

## Why yours looks like everyone else's

There is a remarkably consistent pattern across Centreville, Ashburn, Burke, Gainesville and everywhere in between. Families move in, the garage holds two cars for a few years, and then it slowly stops.

The contents converge too. Four bicycles in ascending sizes, at least two with flat tyres. A treadmill functioning primarily as a clothes rack. Paint tins from three different repaints, none of which match anything currently on a wall. Holiday decorations in a dozen plastic bins. Sports equipment for a sport nobody in the house has played since 2019. A chest freezer that may or may not still run. And a wall of storage tubs whose contents nobody can name without opening them.

This is not a personal failing. It is what happens when a household has a large dry storage space and no forcing function to empty it.

## The forcing function

People almost never clear a garage because they decided to. They clear it because of one of three things:

A car needs to come inside before winter. The house is going on the market. Or a parent is moving in and the space has to become something else.

If none of those has happened to you yet, the practical advice is to pick a date anyway, because the alternative is another four years.

## The four-pile method

Do not start by carrying things. Start by sorting, and use four destinations rather than three — the fourth is the one people forget and it is the one with legal consequences.

**Keep.** Goes back on shelves, properly, at the end. If you have not touched it in two years and it is not seasonal or sentimental, it is not this pile.

**Donate.** Working tools, usable sports equipment, functional furniture, undamaged toys. Be realistic — donation partners have genuine standards and refuse more than people expect. Anything damp, mouldy, broken or missing parts will be turned away at the door.

**Haul.** Everything broken, obsolete, or that nobody will take. This is the pile that ends up in a truck.

**Hazardous.** Paint, stain, solvents, motor oil, antifreeze, fuel in a gas can, pool chemicals, weedkiller, propane tanks, car batteries. This pile is legally distinct and it cannot go in the other three.

## The hazardous pile is not optional

This is the single most common thing people get wrong, and it matters more than it sounds.

Those materials cannot go in regular county collection, cannot go in a dumpster, and cannot go in a junk removal truck — not because haulers are being fussy, but because a load contaminated with solvent or fuel is no longer general waste and the whole truck becomes a problem.

The good news is that the fix is easy and free. Every county here runs residential household hazardous waste drop-off:

- **Fairfax County** — the I-66 Transfer Station off West Ox Road, and the I-95 Landfill Complex in Lorton
- **Loudoun County** — the Solid Waste Management Facility outside Leesburg
- **Prince William County** — the landfill complex off Dumfries Road
- **Arlington County** — the county's household hazardous materials programme at the Trades Center

All of them take residential quantities from residents at no charge. Check current hours before you drive out, since several run limited schedules. It is one trip, and it should be the first thing you do rather than the last, because those tins will otherwise sit in the corner of an otherwise-empty garage for another year.

## Half-used paint specifically

Worth its own note, because there is a lot of it.

Latex paint that is genuinely dried solid can usually go in regular waste in most jurisdictions — the standard method is to leave the lid off, or mix in cat litter or a paint hardener, until it is a solid block rather than a liquid. Oil-based paint, stain and solvent cannot, regardless of how dry it looks.

If you are not sure which you have, treat it as hazardous and take it to the drop-off. It costs you nothing.

## What the county will and will not collect

Every jurisdiction here runs some form of bulk collection, and if what you have is modest, you can get it kerbside, and you can wait for the schedule, use it. You are already paying for it.

Where it stops working is predictable: there are caps on how much goes out per collection, appliances with refrigerant are excluded or need a separate arrangement, mattresses have their own restrictions, construction and demolition debris is not accepted at all, and everything has to reach the kerb under its own power.

That last constraint is usually the real one. Getting a chest freezer from the back of a garage to the street is the entire difficulty.

## A realistic weekend plan

**Friday evening.** Load the hazardous pile into your car. Nothing else.

**Saturday morning, early.** Drop the hazardous material at the county site. Then pull everything — genuinely everything — out onto the driveway. This feels insane and it is the only way it works, because you cannot sort what you cannot see.

**Saturday, rest of the day.** Sort into the remaining three piles. This is the slow part. Expect it to take most of the day and expect to be more ruthless by hour four than you were at hour one, which is normal and correct.

**Saturday evening.** Book the haul for Sunday or Monday. Put the keep pile back on shelves properly rather than in a heap, because otherwise you have simply rearranged the problem.

**Sunday.** Donation run, then the haul. A truck arrives, the pile goes, and you sweep a floor you have not seen in a decade.

## What it costs to have the pile taken

A packed two-car garage is typically a half or three-quarter load — $389 to $499, or $539 to $649. If it is stacked to the ceiling with a full storage room behind it, closer to a full load at $679 to $795.

Doing it yourself is genuinely cheaper only if you already own a truck. A rental truck plus fuel plus per-ton disposal fees at the county transfer station plus three trips across a weekend usually lands close to the hauling price, and costs you the weekend as well.

The one thing worth doing yourself either way is the hazardous run. Nobody can do that part for you.
