## A planned community with the same bones as Reston

Burke Centre was developed through the 1970s and into the 1980s on a philosophy borrowed fairly directly from Reston: cluster the housing, share the green space, keep the cars at the edges, and run pathways between everything.

It works. Burke Centre is genuinely pleasant to live in and the tree cover is excellent. It is also, for anyone carrying a sofa, a specific set of problems.

The community is organised into five conservancies, each with its own common land and its own character, and a substantial number of units are reachable only on foot from a shared parking area. Some back onto wooded pathways with no vehicle approach on that side at all.

## What pathway access actually means

If your unit is one of the pathway-access ones, the practical reality is that everything travels a distance on foot — sometimes thirty yards, sometimes considerably more, occasionally down steps and around a bend under trees.

We plan for it. It changes how many people we bring and how long the job takes, and it is the main reason we ask which conservancy you are in when you book rather than just taking the street address.

What it does not change is your price. This is the single clearest illustration of why volume pricing beats hourly pricing: an hourly hauler doing a Burke Centre pathway carry is being paid more precisely because your home is harder to reach, which is a strange thing to bill a customer for.

## The downsizing wave

Burke's original buyers arrived in the late seventies and early eighties, largely federal workers and military families drawn by the schools and the price relative to inside-the-Beltway.

A great many of them stayed for the whole run. Which means Burke is now in a sustained wave of downsizing — houses too large after the children left, stairs becoming a consideration, and a move to a condo or to be near grandchildren somewhere else.

Downsizing work is different from clearing work, and we treat it differently. The customer is not throwing everything out; they are keeping a curated fraction, want a meaningful amount donated rather than dumped, and are making a large number of decisions under time pressure. So we sort in that order — keep, donate, haul — rather than backing a truck up and filling it.

If a charitable deduction matters for your taxes, say so before we load and we will get you itemised receipts.

## Lake Braddock and the detached side

Not all of Burke is townhouses. Lake Braddock, Longwood Knolls, Signal Hill and Kings Park West are largely detached single-family homes from the same era, on proper lots with garages, sheds and back yards.

Those generate the more familiar Fairfax County job list: garage clearings, basement storage rooms, deck tear-outs, shed removals and play sets that outlived the children. The access is normal, the truck gets to the driveway, and these are straightforward same-day work.

## The trees, again

Burke shares Reston's heavy canopy and gets the same consequences. Decks and fences rot faster in permanent shade. Wooden sheds have shorter lives. Summer storms bring down limbs from mature oaks standing over forty-year-old houses.

Yard waste and storm debris are a bigger share of Burke work than of, say, Centreville work, where the tree cover is younger and thinner. After a significant storm we prioritise anything blocking a driveway or a pathway — which in a cluster community can strand more than one household at a time.
