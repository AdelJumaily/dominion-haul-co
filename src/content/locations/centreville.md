## A suburb that was built all at once

Centreville went from a rural crossroads on Route 29 to a substantial suburb in roughly fifteen years. The bulk of it — Little Rocky Run, Sully Station, Virginia Run, Compton Village — was developed between the mid-1980s and the late 1990s.

Building an entire area in one burst produces a hauling pattern you can almost set a calendar by. Thousands of households moved in within a few years of each other, raised families on roughly the same timeline, and are now hitting the same life stages simultaneously: kids grown and gone, houses too big, garages full of the last twenty years.

That is why our Centreville work is so consistently the same two jobs.

## The garage

This is the defining Centreville call, and it is remarkably uniform.

Two-car attached garage. Has not fitted a car since somewhere around 2009. Contains, in some order: a chest freezer that may or may not still run, four bicycles with flat tyres, a treadmill functioning as a clothes rack, paint tins from three different repaints, holiday decorations in a dozen bins, sports equipment for a sport nobody in the house plays anymore, and a wall of plastic storage tubs whose contents no one can name.

Usually a half or three-quarter load. Usually four hours. Almost always the trigger is one of three things — a car that needs to come inside before winter, a house going on the market, or a parent moving in.

## The basement storage room

The companion job. Nearly every home of this era in Centreville has an unfinished storage area behind the finished basement, and it operates as an archive.

What is in there tends to be older and more sentimental than garage contents: baby furniture, boxed college textbooks, filing boxes of tax records from the nineties, the CRT television that got replaced but not disposed of, and furniture from a previous house that was too good to throw out and never used again.

These clear more slowly than garages, because more decisions get made per box.

## Back yards, and children who grew up

The other Centreville constant is outdoor. These are family lots that raised a full cohort of children who are now in their twenties and thirties, and the evidence is still standing in the back yard.

Wooden play sets are the big one — a cedar swing set that has been out in the weather for eighteen years is well past safe, and it is not coming apart without tools. We dismantle on site. Trampolines, basketball hoops, above-ground pools and the accumulated patio furniture of two decades round out the category.

Anything set in concrete is worth flagging when you call, because a basketball post in a footing is a different job than one on a portable base.

## HOAs, and why hauling beats a container

Centreville is close to universally HOA-governed, and the covenants are generally enforced.

Practically, that rules out the dumpster approach for most people. A container in a Centreville driveway is visible, most associations restrict how long one may sit, and several require advance approval. Same-day hauling sidesteps the entire question, because nothing remains on the property when we leave.

We also keep the truck on your driveway rather than the street, which is the most common association rule about vehicles like ours, and we work standard daytime hours.

## Scheduling

Centreville is roughly fifteen minutes from our Chantilly base, comfortably inside same-day range for weekday morning bookings.

Saturdays are the busiest day here by a wide margin — a garage clearing is a weekend project for most households, and everyone reaches that conclusion at once. Book a Saturday a few days ahead if you can. Weekday afternoons are wide open and get you a faster crew.
