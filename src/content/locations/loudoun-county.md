## Two counties wearing one name

Loudoun does not really behave like a single place, and the hauling work reflects that split precisely.

East of Route 15 is one of the fastest-growing suburban areas in the United States. Ashburn, Brambleton, South Riding, Broadlands and the Dulles corridor were farmland within living memory and are now dense residential development wrapped around the largest concentration of data centres on the planet. Housing here is overwhelmingly built after 1995 — big garages, wide streets, walk-out basements, HOA covenants on everything.

West of Leesburg is a different county entirely. Purcellville, Round Hill, Hamilton, Lovettsville and Middleburg sit in genuine horse and farm country, with housing stock that ranges from 1990s subdivisions to farmhouses that predate the Civil War. Outbuildings, barns, equipment sheds and properties that have had the same family in them for generations.

## What each half calls us for

Eastern Loudoun is a garage-and-basement county. The homes are young enough that nothing has failed yet, but old enough that the original owners have accumulated fifteen or twenty years of belongings. The classic call is a two-car garage in Ashburn or South Riding that has not held a car since 2011, plus the basement storage room behind it. That is usually a half or three-quarter load.

The other eastern-Loudoun staple is basement finishing debris. A great many homes here were built with unfinished walk-out basements, and a large share of them are being finished now — which generates drywall scrap, offcut lumber, old carpet and packaging in reliable quantity.

Western Loudoun is barns, sheds and decades. Farm properties accumulate differently: equipment that stopped working in the nineties, fencing, lumber piles, outbuildings that have rotted past saving. Shed and outbuilding demolition is a much bigger share of our western Loudoun work than it is anywhere else in the region.

## The drive is real, and we do not bill you for it

Purcellville to our base in Chantilly is around forty minutes each way in good traffic, and Route 7 through Leesburg at the wrong hour is not good traffic.

We absorb that. Loudoun pricing is identical to Fairfax and Arlington pricing, with no distance surcharge and no fuel line item. What the drive does affect is scheduling honesty — for anything west of Leesburg we will generally offer you a next-morning slot rather than pretend we can be there this afternoon on top of an existing day.

## Data centre country has a side effect

Worth knowing if you are in eastern Loudoun: the construction volume around the Dulles corridor means the region's disposal infrastructure runs busy, and the Loudoun County Solid Waste Management Facility outside Leesburg has real queues at peak times.

It does not change your price, because that is our problem rather than yours. It is part of why we prefer to schedule Loudoun work in the morning, so the run to the facility does not land in the worst part of the afternoon.
