## The newest housing we work in

Western Prince William went from working farmland to substantial suburb in about twenty years. Heathcote, Piedmont, Dominion Valley, Braemar and Somerset Crossing were largely built from the late 1990s onward, with the Virginia Gateway commercial development arriving alongside them.

That makes Gainesville and Haymarket the youngest housing stock in our whole service area, and it shows in the work. Nothing here needs clearing because it is failing. Roofs are sound, decks are relatively young, and the appliance-replacement wave that dominates Sterling Park work has barely started here.

What these houses generate instead is the accumulation of a decade or two of family life in homes with a lot of storage capacity — and a great deal of basement finishing debris.

## Big houses, big garages, big loads

Homes out here are larger on average than anywhere else we serve, and they were built with the storage to match: three-car garages in places, generous unfinished basements, walk-up attics, and lots with room for sheds and outbuildings.

The practical consequence is straightforward. When a Gainesville garage finally gets cleared, it is more often a three-quarter or full load than the half load a Centreville townhouse garage produces. Same job, more volume.

Back yards follow the pattern too. Larger lots meant larger play sets, trampolines, above-ground pools and sheds — all of which are now hitting the age where they come out.

## Basement build-outs, everywhere

This is the signature job of western Prince William, for the same reason it dominates in Ashburn: a very large share of these homes were sold with unfinished basement space that owners are now finishing.

The debris is predictable — drywall offcuts, framing lumber ends, packaging from flooring and fixtures, and old carpet where any existed. The part people underestimate is everything that was being stored down there before the project could start, which is frequently the larger volume of the two.

We schedule recurring pickups across a multi-week build rather than one heap at the end, so the space stays workable and material never blocks the next trade.

Two things we quote separately: concrete and tile, because disposal for those is billed by weight rather than volume, and we would rather tell you that before loading than surprise you afterwards.

## HOAs are close to universal here

Practically every community in Gainesville, Haymarket and Bristow is HOA-governed, and the covenants tend to be detailed and enforced.

Common provisions affect us directly: where a commercial vehicle may park, what hours work may take place, and whether a vendor needs advance notification. We keep the truck on your driveway rather than the street, we work standard daytime hours, and if your association wants notice, tell us and we will fit their window.

It is also the main reason dumpster rental is impractical for most people here. A container in a Dominion Valley or Piedmont driveway will typically need written approval, has a maximum permitted duration, and is visible to everyone. Same-day hauling avoids the whole conversation, since nothing remains on the property once we drive away.

## The edge of the map, honestly

Gainesville and Haymarket are the furthest points in our service area — roughly forty-five minutes from our Chantilly base with I-66 cooperating, which it frequently does not.
